# Aurex Website Quality Scorecard

This is an internal judgment framework, not a scientific measurement system. Scores help reveal weak areas and prevent the team from overvaluing one impressive feature while ignoring fundamentals.

## Scoring scale

- 1-3: materially weak
- 4-5: functional but below Aurex standard
- 6: competent
- 7: strong
- 8: premium / clearly above ordinary SMB execution
- 9: exceptional
- 10: rare, category-leading execution

Do not inflate scores to finish a project.

## Core dimensions

### 1. Business clarity
Can a relevant visitor quickly understand what the company offers, who it is for, why it matters, and what to do next?

### 2. Conversion architecture
Are actions obvious, contextual, low-friction, credible, measurable, and connected to an effective post-submit or purchase flow?

### 3. Brand specificity
Does the experience feel designed for this company rather than adapted from a generic template?

### 4. Creative concept
Is there an ownable idea rooted in the business, and is it expressed with enough restraint to remain useful?

### 5. Visual hierarchy
Do scale, contrast, spacing, composition, and sequencing direct attention correctly?

### 6. Color system
Does color create identity, hierarchy, atmosphere, and CTA contrast while maintaining accessibility?

### 7. Typography
Is typography readable, intentional, appropriately distinctive, and consistent with the brand?

### 8. Imagery and media
Are visuals doing strategic work through story, proof, emotion, explanation, or product/service demonstration?

### 9. Page rhythm and storytelling
Does the visitor experience a coherent journey instead of a stack of interchangeable sections?

### 10. Motion and interaction
Does motion improve attention, continuity, feedback, narrative, or perceived quality without slowing the experience?

### 11. Content and copy
Is the writing specific, truthful, persuasive, useful, human-sounding, and appropriately aligned with search intent?

### 12. Trust and proof
Are important claims supported near the decision points where proof matters?

### 13. Mobile experience
Does mobile feel intentionally designed, with appropriate hierarchy, forms, imagery, navigation, CTA access, and motion?

### 14. Accessibility and usability
Can people navigate, understand, and operate the experience reliably across common input and accessibility needs?

### 15. Organic visibility foundation
Does the architecture and implementation support useful indexable content, semantic structure, internal linking, local/topical relevance, and sound technical SEO?

### 16. Performance and engineering quality
Does the site feel fast and stable, use media responsibly, avoid unnecessary complexity, and pass production requirements?

### 17. Detail and polish
Are microcopy, spacing, states, icons, transitions, errors, forms, focus treatment, image crops, and edge cases considered?

## Approval rules

A website should not receive final Aurex approval when:

- any BLOCKER remains
- the primary conversion flow is untested
- mobile contains known major failures
- accessibility has known material barriers that can reasonably be fixed
- the site materially diverges from the approved strategy or concept without an explicit decision
- key business claims are unsupported or inaccurate
- essential SEO migration/indexability requirements are unresolved
- the production build is materially broken

As an internal target, core dimensions should generally be strong rather than merely functional. A high average score cannot compensate for a serious weakness in business clarity, conversion, accessibility, mobile, or technical correctness.

## Creative-director question

After scoring, ignore the numbers for a moment and ask:

"Would a strong prospective client see this and believe Aurex brought strategy, creative direction, development craft, and growth thinking to the project?"

If the answer is uncertain, identify why before approving the work.
