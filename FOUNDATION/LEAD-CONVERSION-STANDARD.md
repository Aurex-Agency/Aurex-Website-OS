# Aurex Lead Conversion Standard

Aurex websites should make it as easy as reasonably possible for a qualified visitor to become a lead.

The goal is not simply to place more forms or buttons on a page. The goal is to remove unnecessary friction while increasing clarity, trust, relevance, and momentum.

## 1. Define the conversion before designing the interface

For each page, identify:

- primary conversion
- secondary conversion
- visitor intent
- visitor readiness
- information the business truly needs at first contact
- objections that may block action
- reassurance or proof that may reduce hesitation
- what should happen immediately after conversion

A service page, emergency page, financing page, and general contact page may require different conversion patterns.

## 2. Minimize unnecessary friction

Every requested field creates some amount of effort, uncertainty, or perceived commitment.

Do not ask for information at first contact simply because it may be useful later.

Classify fields as:

- required to respond
- useful for routing or qualification
- useful later
- unnecessary

Prefer collecting only what is needed to complete the current step.

## 3. Choose the form pattern based on the job

Aurex may use:

- compact single-step forms
- conversational step-by-step forms
- multi-step qualification flows
- appointment booking
- quote builders
- calculators
- click-to-call
- click-to-text
- embedded scheduling
- ecommerce or application flows

Do not assume one pattern is universally superior.

### Single-step forms are often appropriate when

- the request is simple
- only a few fields are required
- urgency is high
- the user already understands the service

### Multi-step forms may be appropriate when

- the request naturally breaks into understandable stages
- qualification genuinely helps the user or business
- later questions depend on earlier answers
- progressive disclosure makes a complex process easier to understand

Do not use multiple steps merely to hide the true length of an excessive form.

## 4. Make the first step easy

When a multi-step experience is appropriate, the opening interaction should feel low-risk and relevant.

Examples:

- What can we help you with?
- What type of project are you planning?
- Where is the property located?
- Choose the service you need.

Do not begin with high-friction questions unless they are necessary.

## 5. Explain the value of completing the form

The conversion area should answer:

- What will I get?
- What happens next?
- How quickly should I expect a response?
- Am I committing to anything?
- Why should I trust this company with my information?

Use specific CTA language when a more meaningful action is available.

Examples:

- Request My Estimate
- Schedule My Visit
- Check Availability
- Start My Project

Avoid forcing clever copy when a simple label is clearer.

## 6. Place conversion opportunities at moments of readiness

Aurex commonly considers CTA opportunities after:

- the main promise
- a service explanation
- meaningful proof
- a case study
- pricing or financing clarification
- objection handling
- process explanation
- location or availability information

Repeated conversion access can be useful, but each placement should feel contextually earned.

## 7. Use trust near the decision

Useful conversion-area reassurance may include:

- response-time expectation
- no-obligation language when accurate
- privacy reassurance
- rating or review evidence
- relevant guarantee
- years in business
- license or certification
- local presence
- recognizable proof

Do not overload the form with every trust badge available.

## 8. Design mobile conversion intentionally

On mobile, verify:

- input types summon the correct keyboard
- fields are comfortable to tap
- labels remain visible and clear
- errors are understandable
- phone and text actions work correctly
- sticky CTA behavior does not obstruct content
- multi-step flows preserve progress appropriately
- keyboard appearance does not break the layout

## 9. Engineer the post-submit experience

Submission is not the end of the UX.

Define:

- confirmation state
- expected response time
- next action
- scheduling opportunity
- phone or text fallback
- CRM delivery
- owner or sales notification
- analytics event
- advertising conversion event where appropriate
- duplicate-submission behavior
- failure and retry behavior

A successful form that silently loses the lead is a failed conversion system.

## 10. Measure before claiming optimization

Where traffic volume and project scope allow, improve conversion decisions using real evidence.

Potential evidence includes:

- form starts
- step completion
- abandonment points
- form submissions
- qualified lead rate
- booked appointment rate
- call conversions
- source and landing page
- device differences
- user research
- session behavior
- controlled experiments

Do not call a pattern "high converting" merely because it looks like patterns used on other websites.

## 11. Lead quality still matters

Lower friction is valuable only if the business can respond to and benefit from the resulting leads.

Balance completion rate with:

- qualification needs
- spam risk
- service area
- minimum project requirements
- scheduling constraints
- sales capacity

Where qualification is necessary, make it feel helpful and understandable rather than bureaucratic.

## 12. Definition of done

A primary lead flow is not complete until it has been tested end to end.

Verify:

- all fields work
- validation works
- required fields are actually necessary
- mobile completion works
- success state works
- failure state works
- CRM or destination receives the data
- relevant notifications fire
- tracking events fire
- spam protection is reasonable
- conversion copy matches what happens next
