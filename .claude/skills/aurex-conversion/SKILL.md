# Aurex Conversion

Read `FOUNDATION/LEAD-CONVERSION-STANDARD.md` for lead-generation projects.

## Principle

Make the next useful action obvious and easy without turning the website into an aggressive funnel.

## Analyze

- primary and secondary conversions
- visitor intent and readiness by page
- value proposition clarity
- offer and risk-reversal clarity
- objections and uncertainty
- proof required near important claims
- CTA hierarchy and placement
- contact-path availability
- form length and field necessity
- single-step vs progressive/multi-step fit
- mobile completion friction
- post-submit state and expectation setting
- lead routing and follow-up
- analytics and advertising conversion events

## Form rule

Classify every field as:

- required now
- useful for qualification now
- useful later
- unnecessary

Challenge unnecessary friction.

Do not assume multi-step or short forms always win. Match the flow to complexity, urgency, qualification needs, trust, and available evidence.

## Conversion narrative

For key pages, map the sequence:

Visitor question -> message -> proof -> objection resolution -> action.

Repeated CTAs should appear at earned moments of readiness.

## Output

Create or update a conversion section in the project strategy or `CONVERSION-PLAN.md` containing:

1. conversion hierarchy
2. page-level conversion roles
3. objection/proof matrix
4. CTA system
5. primary lead or purchase flow
6. field rationale
7. trust/reassurance near conversion
8. mobile conversion behavior
9. post-submit experience
10. tracking plan
11. hypotheses worth testing
12. risks and unresolved questions

## Evidence

Label CRO claims that are project hypotheses as hypotheses. Use current research or project data when a decision materially depends on a generalized conversion claim.

## QA

A lead flow is not complete until tested end to end from interaction through destination, success/failure states, notifications, and tracking.
