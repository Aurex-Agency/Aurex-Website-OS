# Aurex Discovery

Purpose: understand the business deeply enough that strategy and creative direction are specific rather than generic.

## Inputs

Read the project brief, current website/code, supplied assets, brand materials, and prior approved decisions.

## Research

Investigate as relevant:

- what the business actually sells and how it makes money
- highest-value and highest-priority offers
- primary and secondary audiences
- customer problems, desired outcomes, language, objections, and decision criteria
- geography and local context
- competitors and alternatives
- category conventions and opportunities to break them intelligently
- existing brand identity and reputation
- proof assets such as reviews, case studies, credentials, results, projects, team expertise, warranties, locations, and history
- current website strengths, weaknesses, content gaps, and conversion friction
- search and content opportunities
- creative raw material: objects, materials, processes, terminology, physical environment, logo forms, history, transformations, and visual moments
- asset quality and missing photography/video

## Evidence discipline

Separate findings into:

- VERIFIED FACT
- STRONG EVIDENCE
- REASONABLE INFERENCE
- OPEN QUESTION

Do not turn assumptions into brand claims.

For current external facts, use current sources. Prefer the business itself, official sources, primary documentation, public records where appropriate, and strong direct evidence. Use competitor marketing as evidence of competitor positioning, not as proof that a claim is objectively true.

## Required synthesis

Produce or update `DISCOVERY.md` with:

1. Executive summary
2. Business model and priority offers
3. Audience and visitor intents
4. Customer decision drivers
5. Objections and trust requirements
6. Positioning landscape
7. Competitor patterns and weaknesses
8. Proof inventory
9. Content and asset inventory
10. Organic visibility opportunities
11. Conversion opportunities
12. Business-native creative territory
13. Risks, constraints, and unknowns
14. Recommended strategic opportunities ranked by impact

## Quality bar

The output should teach an experienced strategist something useful. Avoid obvious statements such as "the site should be mobile friendly" unless tied to a project-specific issue.

Prioritize insights that change what Aurex should build, say, show, measure, or avoid.

Before completion ask: what did we learn that a generic agency would probably miss?
