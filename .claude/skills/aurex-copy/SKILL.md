# Aurex Website Copy

Copy should sound like this business, not like an AI's idea of a business.

## Inputs

Use discovery, positioning, real customer language, offers, proof, objections, search intent, page purpose, and approved creative direction.

Never invent testimonials, credentials, outcomes, statistics, guarantees, years in business, pricing, or differentiators.

## Message hierarchy

For every important page define:

- what the visitor needs to understand first
- the strongest truthful promise
- why it matters
- why this business is credible
- what objection should be answered next
- what proof supports the claim
- what action should follow

## Writing standard

Prefer:

- specific nouns and verbs
- concrete outcomes
- short clear sentences mixed with intentional longer ones
- customer language when accurate
- proof close to the claim it supports
- headlines that communicate, not merely decorate
- CTA language that reflects the action or value

Avoid:

- vague superlatives
- "elevate," "unlock," "redefine," and similar AI-marketing filler unless genuinely natural for the brand
- repetitive three-part slogans
- unsupported claims
- keyword stuffing
- identical sentence cadence across sections
- generic local-business clichés
- copy that could be swapped onto a competitor unchanged
- en dashes and em dashes in user-facing copy

## SEO

Satisfy search intent naturally. Important terms should appear where useful because the page is genuinely about them, not because a density target demands it.

## Conversion

Write CTA and form microcopy to reduce uncertainty. Where accurate, clarify what happens next, response expectations, commitment level, or required information.

## Creative direction

Allow the approved concept to influence language lightly when it strengthens memory or narrative. Do not turn every line into a metaphor.

## Review test

Before approval ask:

- is the core offer clear within seconds?
- are claims specific and supportable?
- does the writing sound human?
- could a competitor use this unchanged?
- is proof doing enough work?
- are we explaining features when we should explain customer value?
- is the CTA natural for the visitor's readiness?

Rewrite weak copy rather than decorating it with adjectives.
