# Aurex Site Architecture

Use after discovery and positioning are sufficiently clear.

## Goal

Create the smallest useful architecture that fully serves important visitor intents, conversion paths, proof needs, and organic opportunities.

Do not inflate page count for SEO. Do not compress genuinely different intents into one overloaded page.

## For each proposed page define

- page name and URL intent
- primary audience or visitor intent
- job the page must accomplish
- primary message
- primary conversion
- secondary conversion if relevant
- proof required
- major content sections
- search intent where relevant
- parent/child relationship
- important internal links in and out
- whether the page needs unique imagery, video, data, or interactive elements

## Page grouping

Organize the sitemap into practical bundles such as:

- core commercial pages
- services or products
- trust and proof
- locations or service areas where useful
- resources and education
- conversion flows
- legal and utility

The actual bundles must follow the business rather than this list mechanically.

## SEO guardrails

Reject or merge pages when they would be thin, duplicative, doorway-like, or unable to satisfy a distinct intent.

Preserve valuable existing URLs when redesigning a live site unless a deliberate redirect/consolidation strategy is documented.

## Navigation

Design navigation around customer understanding, not internal company structure. Keep important conversion paths obvious. Consider desktop and mobile separately.

## Output

Create or update `SITE-ARCHITECTURE.md` containing:

1. architecture rationale
2. sitemap
3. page purpose matrix
4. page bundles
5. navigation model
6. internal linking strategy
7. SEO/content opportunities
8. consolidation or redirect notes
9. content/assets required per page group
10. open decisions

## Human gate

Bring the architecture to the human creative lead with a recommended sitemap, the biggest strategic choices, and any pages whose value is uncertain.
