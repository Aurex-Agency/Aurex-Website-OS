# Aurex Research

Use this skill when a material website decision should be grounded in evidence rather than taste or memory.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the question:

1. primary documentation, standards, official platform guidance, and first-party data
2. peer-reviewed or rigorous published research
3. reputable industry studies with transparent methodology
4. direct project analytics and user evidence
5. high-quality expert analysis
6. anecdotal examples only as inspiration, never as universal proof

For competitor research, the competitor's own site is authoritative for what they claim and how they position themselves, not for whether their claims are true.

## Research behavior

- verify time-sensitive claims with current sources
- distinguish correlation from causation
- inspect study population, sample size, date, methodology, and relevance when available
- do not elevate a single case study into a universal rule
- identify disagreement between credible sources
- state uncertainty when evidence is weak
- distinguish best practice, reasonable heuristic, experiment, and aesthetic preference

## Output format

For material research, provide:

### Decision question
What are we trying to decide?

### Evidence
Concise findings with sources or project evidence.

### Confidence
High / Medium / Low, with reason.

### Recommendation
What Aurex should do for this specific project.

### Tradeoffs
What the recommendation may cost or compromise.

### Validation plan
How to test or measure the decision when uncertainty remains.

## CRO warning

Do not claim that a button color, form pattern, CTA count, hero format, navigation style, or other pattern is "high converting" without relevant evidence.

## SEO warning

Do not recommend tactics solely because an SEO tool awards points. Search intent, usefulness, crawlability, architecture, content quality, technical correctness, and current search-engine guidance matter more than arbitrary checklist scores.
